1. What ₹35 LPA Actually Means

₹35 LPA at three-and-a-half years is SDE-2 at a tier-1 Indian product company. It is reachable from where you are, but it is not the median outcome — it is roughly the 80th percentile of that cohort. Getting there is a function of four things, and only one of them is on your resume today.

ComponentTypical shareWhat it means for you
Fixed base₹24 – 28 LThe number that pays your bills. Negotiate this hardest.
ESOP / RSU (annualised)₹4 – 8 LStartups quote 4-year totals to inflate the headline. Always divide by 4.
Performance variable₹2 – 4 LUsually 10% of base, paid at 80–100%. Treat 90% as the realistic figure.
Joining bonus₹1 – 3 LOne-time. Never let it be counted as “CTC”.
Read every offer this way

A ₹35 L “CTC” that is ₹19 L base + ₹40 L ESOP over 4 years + ₹3 L joining is really ₹19 L of guaranteed money. A ₹31 L offer at ₹27 L base is the better job. Ask for the written breakup before you react to any number.

The core insight

Your engineering depth is already above your years. The problem is that it is invisible (no numbers on anything) and unproven (no DSA, no scale). Both are fixable in about eight months. Neither requires abandoning what you have built — the plan below converts your existing embedded/Linux work into cloud vocabulary rather than replacing it.

One title risk to manage

“Senior Software Engineer” at 2.5 years is Delta’s hardware-industry title scale. Product-company recruiters read it as inflated and it can trigger distrust or the wrong (higher) bar. Keep the title on the resume — it is real — but in outreach and interviews always lead with years and scope, never with the word “senior”.

The rule that makes this work

DSA runs every single day from week 1 to the last interview. It is the long pole and the only truly non-negotiable track. Everything else is layered on top of it. Two problems a day for eight months is ~480 problems — far more than you need. Consistency beats intensity; a two-week gap costs you a month of re-learning.

Week 1  ·  ~8 hours total Phase 0 Resume Surgery & Baseline

Free wins. These cost one weekend and raise your callback rate before you have learned anything new.

Step 0.1 — Put a number on every bullet

Not one figure appears in the current resume. You have the data; you simply did not write it down. Go find these and put them in:

  • PDU Gen-3: how many units shipped / in the field? How many concurrent touch panels and admin sessions? The 5-second sampler over a one-hour window = how many datapoints held in memory, mirrored to disk how often? How many alarm types? What is the panel’s resolution and frame budget? How large is the ARM64 binary and how long does the installer take end to end?

Where a count is genuinely small, use a hard constraint instead — latency, uptime, protocol timing, memory ceiling. “Held p95 under 120 ms on a 512 MB ARM board” is stronger than any user count.

The Capstone: “Habex at Scale”

Why upgrade rather than start fresh

A new side project restarts your credibility from zero. Habex is already live, on your own domain, with 12 migrations and 3 edge functions. Scaling it closes all four blocking gaps at once — distributed systems, cloud/DevOps, observability, and measurable numbers — on a product you can already speak about with authority. One project, four gaps.

Ship these seven things by the end of month 5:

  1. Containerised API on AWS — Docker image on EC2 behind an ALB, RDS Postgres, ElastiCache Redis, TLS on a subdomain.
  2. Redis caching + rate limiting — cache-aside on the heaviest read path with a documented invalidation strategy; per-user token-bucket limiting.
  3. BullMQ job queue — recurring expenses, notifications and monthly rollups run asynchronously with retries and a dead-letter queue.
  4. Idempotency + audit trail — every write carries an idempotency key; every privileged action lands in an immutable audit log.
  5. CI/CD pipeline — GitHub Actions running tests and deploying on merge to main, with a documented rollback path.
  6. Observability — structured logs with correlation IDs, a metrics endpoint, Sentry, and one alert that actually fires.
  7. A load-test report — k6 run at 200 concurrent users, p50/p95/p99 before and after optimisation, written up in the README with a diagram.

Then write one architecture document in the repo — a request-flow diagram, the caching and invalidation strategy, the queue topology, and a short “what I would do at 100× traffic” section. That document is what you screen-share in a system design round, and it is the single highest-leverage artefact in this entire plan.

Track D — System design & LLD (start month 4, 3× per week)

You already have a curriculum underway at skill/system-design/. Reweight it: at three years of experience, LLD is roughly 70% of what you will be asked and HLD is 30%. Most candidates over-prepare distributed HLD and then fail an object-modelling round.

LLD — be fluent in these ten, out loud, on a whiteboard

Rate limiter (all four algorithms) · in-memory cache with LRU + TTL · URL shortener · Splitwise / expense splitting · parking lot · elevator · notification service with multiple channels · payment idempotency layer · tic-tac-toe / chess board model · logging library. Practise SOLID, composition over inheritance, and the strategy/factory/observer patterns as they appear in these problems — not as abstract theory.

HLD — the concepts you must be able to draw and defend

Load balancing (L4 vs L7) · caching layers and invalidation · DB replication and read replicas · sharding and partition keys · CAP and what you trade · consistent hashing · message queues and back-pressure · CDN · rate limiting at the edge · idempotency · eventual consistency · the outbox pattern. Then practise five full designs: Instagram feed, WhatsApp, Uber matching, a payment gateway, and a Zepto-style 10-minute delivery/inventory system.

Practise out loud, with a timer

Silent reading does not transfer to a design round. Record yourself doing a 40-minute design and watch it back once. It is uncomfortable and it is the fastest single improvement available in this plan.

Months 6 – 7  ·  ~15 hrs/week Phase 3 Interview Craft

Track E — Machine coding (Zepto, Swiggy, Flipkart, Meesho)

A 90-minute round where you build a working feature from scratch. They grade working software first, then structure, then edge cases. Do ten of these, timed, no interruptions:

Infinite-scroll feed with a skeleton loader · autocomplete with debounce and request cancellation · nested comment thread with reply/collapse · kanban board with drag-and-drop · multi-step form with validation · data table with sort, filter and pagination · a poll widget with live results · an image carousel · a todo app with optimistic updates and rollback · a file-tree explorer.

Scoring habits: ship something working by minute 60, then improve. Extract components early. Handle loading, empty and error states — most candidates forget all three. Narrate your trade-offs as you type.

Track F — JavaScript & TypeScript depth

You claim TypeScript heavily on the resume, so expect it probed hard.

  • JS internals: event loop, macro vs microtasks, closures, this binding, prototypal inheritance, hoisting and the temporal dead zone.
  • Implement from scratch: Promise.all / race / allSettled, debounce, throttle, curry, deep clone, memoize, an event emitter, map/filter/reduce, a retry-with-backoff wrapper.
  • Advanced TS: generics with constraints, conditional and mapped types, discriminated unions, infer, template literal types, satisfies, and type-safe API contracts shared between client and server.
  • React internals: reconciliation and keys, when memo/useMemo backfires, virtualisation for long lists, Suspense and error boundaries, concurrent rendering, custom hooks, and why your Redux vs Zustand choice went the way it did.

Track G — Behavioural & narrative

At the ₹35 L band the behavioural round is a real filter, not a formality. Write out and rehearse six STAR stories from your actual work:

  1. The hardest technical decision you made alone (sole ownership of PDU Gen-3 — pick one architectural call).
  2. A production bug you shipped and how you found it.
  3. A time you disagreed with a stakeholder or a spec.
  4. Something you built that failed or was rewritten.
  5. How you learn a new technology under deadline.
  6. Why you are leaving hardware for a product company — the most important one. The answer is “I want to build for scale and for millions of users, and to grow alongside a large engineering team”, never “my current job is bad”. Never criticise Delta.

Mock interviews — the highest-ROI hours in the plan

Book at least five before applying: two DSA, two system design, one behavioural. Use Pramp / interviewing.io / peers in your network. A mock exposes in 45 minutes what 40 hours of solo study will not: that you go silent when stuck, or jump to code before clarifying, or cannot estimate complexity out loud.

Sequencing matters — you get one shot per company

Failed loops carry a 6–12 month cooldown. Interview the calibration tier first, then strong-fit, then primary targets, then reach — in that order. Do not apply to CRED in month 3 “just to see”.

Referrals beat everything

  • Start now, not in month 8. A referral roughly triples your callback rate, and cold applications from a hardware company are the ones recruiters filter out first.
  • Build in public from month 1. Post the PDU architecture (nothing confidential), the Habex scale upgrade, and what you learn each month. Eight months of visible building makes referral requests easy instead of awkward.
  • Ask specifically: a short message naming the exact role and one line on why you fit converts far better than “can you refer me”.
  • Reconnect with SIT alumni and ex-Delta colleagues now, while you want nothing from them.

Negotiation — where ₹28 L becomes ₹35 L

  1. Compress your timeline. Apply everywhere inside a two-week window so offers land together. A single offer has almost no leverage; three offers in one week has enormous leverage.
  2. Never state your current CTC first. “I’m targeting a market-rate SDE-2 package and I’m optimising for the role — what band is this position budgeted at?”
  3. Anchor above target. Ask for ₹40–42 L when you want ₹35 L. Recruiters expect to negotiate; your first number is a ceiling, never a floor.
  4. Negotiate base hardest, then joining bonus, then ESOP. Base compounds into every future raise and every future offer.
  5. Get the written breakup before responding — base / variable / ESOP vesting schedule and cliff / joining bonus and its clawback period.
  6. Say it plainly and stop talking: “I have a competing offer at ₹X. I’d prefer to join you — can you get to ₹Y?” Then be silent and let them respond.
